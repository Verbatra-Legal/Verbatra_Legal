Frequently asked questions

Contract Playbook — your questions, answered

If your question is not here, write to verbatra.legal@gmail.com and we will add it.

What is a contract playbook?
A playbook is an internal negotiation reference, not a contract itself. For each key clause, it sets out the position you open with, the fallback you can live with, and the red line you will not cross — so whoever is negotiating (even someone without a legal background) can respond to redlines quickly and consistently, instead of deciding each point from scratch under time pressure.
How is this different from the Contract Risk Review tool?
Risk Review scans a draft you already have and flags where it deviates from market-standard terms. The Playbook Creator works before that: it builds your negotiating position in advance, for the contract type and side you will be on, so you know what to ask for and what to concede before the other side's draft even arrives.
Why does my "side" change the playbook?
Almost every clause in a commercial contract benefits one party more than the other — a liability cap that protects a service provider is the same clause a client wants to loosen. The playbook is built from your side of the table, so a provider and a client negotiating the same MSA get materially different preferred positions, fallbacks and red lines.
What do Conservative, Balanced and Aggressive mean?
They shift where your opening ask and fallback sit on the realistic range for that clause. Conservative opens closer to the fallback and concedes faster — useful when the relationship matters more than the specific term. Aggressive holds the toughest defensible position the longest — useful when you have leverage or the clause is high-stakes. Balanced reflects typical market practice.
Does the playbook change by jurisdiction and industry?
Yes. The governing law, dispute-resolution forum and data-protection regime referenced in the playbook adapt to your chosen jurisdiction (45 supported), and a sector-considerations section is added based on your industry (19 supported) — the same industry logic used in Verbatra's MSA generator.
Do I still need a lawyer to negotiate using this?
For routine, lower-stakes contracts, this playbook is usually enough to negotiate confidently on your own. For high-value deals, regulated sectors, or any clause where the walk-away line is actually being tested by the other side, get a lawyer involved before you concede on it.
